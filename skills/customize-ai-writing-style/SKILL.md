# Customizing AI Writing Style

## Overview
Instructing an AI model like Claude to adopt a specific writing style, including voice, banned words/phrases, sentence structure, and formatting preferences, to make its output sound more human and less 'AI-like'.

**Use case:** Generating content (emails, social posts, articles) that matches a personal or brand voice, avoiding common AI writing patterns.

## Key steps
1. Be direct, have opinions, use specific examples and names, not vague claims.
2. State your point first, then support it.
3. Trust the reader to recognize what matters without labeling it as 'significant' or 'important'.
4. Avoid common AI-writing markers like 'delve', 'pivotal', 'transformative', 'holistic', 'cutting-edge'.
5. Avoid phrases like 'In today's fast-paced world', 'It's important/worth noting that', 'At its core', 'Let's break it down'.
6. Avoid structures like 'It's not just X - it's Y' or 'Not only X, but Y'.
7. Vary paragraph and sentence length; don't write uniform blocks.
8. Don't use 'Bold term: explanation sentence' list format.
9. Don't signpost with phrases like 'Let's explore' or 'Now let's turn to'.
10. Don't open with sweeping contextual statements or close with inspirational wrap-ups.
11. Don't restate the question back before answering it.
12. Use contractions (It's, don't, won't).
13. Use commas or parentheses instead of multiple em dashes.
14. Prefer plain prose over over-formatting with headers and bullet points.
15. Drop preambles, performative enthusiasm, and unsolicited caveats.
16. Match tone to context (casual question, casual answer).
17. Read output aloud to check if it sounds like a press release; rewrite if needed.
18. Avoid repeating the same point in different words.
19. Ensure opening sentences are direct and not grand statements about the state of the world.
20. Consider specific language preferences like British English or metric system.
21. Avoid emojis if a formal or specific tone is desired.
